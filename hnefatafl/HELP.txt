Two unequal sides: the king and his defenders in the middle, twice as many
attackers round the edges. The attackers move first. Every piece moves like
a rook in chess, any number of empty squares along a row or column, never
jumping. You capture an enemy piece by closing it in between two of yours
along a row or column; only the moving side captures. The king escapes to
win; the attackers win by taking him. The rules of each set in short follow;
the full rules, their sources and the history are in RULES.txt and the
rulebook.

Copenhagen 11x11
----------------

- 11x11; 24 attackers against the king and 12 defenders. Attackers first.
- Only the king may stop on the throne and the corners; anyone may pass over
  the empty throne. The king helps capture.
- Corners are hostile to all; the throne always to the attackers, to the
  defenders when empty: they capture like an enemy piece.
- Shield wall: moving onto the edge to close a row of two or more enemy
  pieces along it, each with one of yours in front of it, takes the row (a
  corner may close the far end; the king is never taken this way).
- The king is taken by attackers on all four sides (three beside the
  throne), never on the edge.
- Defenders win when the king reaches a corner, or sits on the edge, able to
  move, in a fort the attackers can never break.
- Attackers win by taking the king or shutting in every defender. The same
  position a third time loses for the defenders; a side that cannot move
  loses.

Fetlar 11x11
------------

- As Copenhagen: board, setup, moves, captures, hostile squares, the ring,
  attackers first.
- No shield wall and no exit fort: the king wins only by a corner.
- The king is taken by attackers on all four sides (three beside the
  throne); on the edge only if he is the last of his side and attackers
  stand on every side of him.
- The same position a third time is a draw. A side that cannot move loses.

Brandubh 7x7
------------

- 7x7; 8 attackers against the king and 4 defenders. Attackers first.
- Moves and captures as Copenhagen; only the king may stop on the throne and
  the corners.
- The king escapes to any corner. Corners are hostile to everyone, the king
  too.
- The king is taken like any piece, between two attackers or an attacker and
  a corner; on the throne it takes all four sides. The empty throne is never
  hostile to him.
- Attackers also win by shutting in every defender. The same position a
  third time loses for whoever made it; a side that cannot move loses.

Tablut 9x9 (Linnaeus)
---------------------

- 9x9; 16 attackers (Muscovites) against the king and 8 defenders (Swedes).
  Attackers first.
- No special corners. Only the king may stop on the throne; anyone may pass
  over it empty. It is hostile to the attackers, and to the defenders when
  empty.
- The king is taken like any piece, between two attackers; on the throne it
  takes four, beside it three with the throne the fourth.
- The king escapes to any square on the edge.
- Attackers also win by shutting in every defender. The same position a
  third time loses for the defenders; a side that cannot move loses.

Tablut 9x9 (Bell 1960)
----------------------

- As Linnaeus’ Tablut: board, setup, moves, captures, the throne, the king
  escaping to the edge, attackers first.
- The king is taken only by attackers on all four sides, or three with the
  empty throne the fourth.
- No ring rule: the attackers win only by taking the king.
- The game calls Raichi! when the king has one clear way to the edge,
  Tuichi! with two.
- The same position a third time is a draw. A side that cannot move loses.

Playing it in the terminal
--------------------------

- Arrows move the cursor; Enter or Space picks up a piece, then puts it on a
  lit square. Or click a piece, then a square. Esc puts it down.
- After a game: Enter or Space plays again; M or Esc goes to the menu.
- M: menu; F1 or ?: this help (Up, Down, PgUp, PgDn scroll); Q: quit.
