Kōnane
Asciiverse
2026-10-23

Every hollow of the board (papamū) is filled with black and white stones in
turn, so each diagonal is of one colour. Two stones are lifted out to begin.
Then a move is a jump: one of your stones leaps an enemy stone next to it,
along a row or a column, into the empty hollow beyond, and takes it; it may
jump on along the same line. Who cannot jump has lost. The rules of each
form in short follow; the full rules, their sources and the history are in
RULES.txt and the rulebook.

Kōnane after Ernst
------------------

- 8 by 8, every hollow filled. Black begins.
- Black lifts one of his stones in the middle (one of the central four) or
  at a corner; White then lifts one of his next to that hollow.
- Then each in turn jumps: over an enemy stone next to his, along a row or
  column, into the empty hollow beyond, taking it. He may jump on in the
  same direction, over stone after stone, and stop when he likes; never
  diagonally, never turning.
- Who cannot jump has lost. Jumping is never forced beyond that.

Modern Kōnane
-------------

- As Kōnane after Ernst, but a move is a single jump: one enemy stone taken,
  never more.

Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau
--------------------

- 9 rows by 11, as the old stone papamū at Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau.
- Black lifts any one of his stones; White any one of his.
- Then jumps as after Ernst: on along a line if you like, never turning. The
  player who makes the last jump wins.

King’s papamū
-------------

- 14 rows by 17: the board of 238 squares, 14 in a row, that James King saw
  in 1779.
- Ernst’s rules, as in the first rule set.

Playing it in the terminal
--------------------------

- Arrows: move the cursor; Enter or Space: lift a lit stone (at the start),
  or pick up a stone and land it on a lit hollow; Esc: let go. Or click.
- The stones a jump would take turn red when the cursor is on its landing.
- When the game is over, Enter plays again.
- M: menu; F1: this help; Q: quit.
