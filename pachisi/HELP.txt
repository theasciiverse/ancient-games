Pachisi and Chaupar
Asciiverse
2026-10-26

A cross of four arms, each three columns by eight rows, round a square in
the middle. Four colours, four men each, one colour to an arm; partners sit
opposite and win together. A man comes out of the middle down the middle row
of its own arm, goes round the outside of the whole cross against the sun,
and back up its own middle row into the middle: 84 steps. Each throw moves
one man its full value. A man landing on a single enemy man sends him back
to the middle, except on the castles (the crossed squares). The partnership
with all eight men home first wins. Pachisi is thrown with six cowrie
shells, chaupar with three long dice. The rules in short follow; the full
rules, the sources and the history are in RULES.txt and the rulebook.

Rules every set shares
----------------------

- Two partnerships: Red and Black against Yellow and Green; you play both
  colours of yours. Red throws first, then Yellow, Black, Green, round
  against the sun (our choice).
- Each throw moves one man its whole value; a throw is never split. Throws
  may be used in any order, on any men, and may be left unused (P leaves the
  rest).
- Landing on a lone enemy man outside a castle sends it back to the middle.
  Partners never take each other, and may share a square.
- Men pass over any men. Going home needs the exact number.

Pachisi, after Tylor
--------------------

- Six cowries; count the mouths up: none 6, one 10, two 2, three 3, four 4,
  five 25, six 12. A 6, 10, 12 or 25 throws again; then the throws are
  played.
- A 10 or a 25 also brings a starting 1: the only way a man enters (onto the
  first square), and the way from square 83 home.
- Two or more men of one colour hold their square: no enemy may land there.
  A castle with an enemy man on it is shut to you.
- Taking a man brings another throw.
- Rotten throws: three high throws (10, 12, 25) running are struck out,
  unless the next throw is a 10 or a 25.
- A colour with all its men home throws for its partner (our choice).

Pachisi, after Falkener
-----------------------

- Six cowries: none up 25, one 10, two 2, three 3, four 4, five 5, six
  6.  A 25, 10 or 6 throws again; a 25 or a 10 brings a grace of 1.
- One man of each colour starts out in the middle; the others come out only
  with a grace. Once out, a man moves from the middle with any throw: 25
  from the middle reaches the castle at the end of the next arm.
- Castles are shut to the enemy. Men may double on other squares at their
  own peril: one enemy man landing there takes them all.
- Taking a man brings another throw. A colour with all its men home simply
  waits.

Chaupar, after Abu’l-Fazl
-------------------------

- Three long dice, each showing 1, 2, 5 or 6. Each die moves one man; no
  throwing again.
- The men start on the board: two on squares 6 and 7 of their middle row,
  two on 15 and 16 of the row on their right.
- A doublet may move two men standing together twice its value each (keeping
  them together); a triplet three. Otherwise dice are used one by one.
- Two or more men together cannot be taken, except by a pair moved together
  by a doublet. There are no castles.
- A man taken goes to the middle and comes back on a die of 1, onto the
  first square (our choice).
- A player with all his men home throws for his partner.

Playing it in the terminal
--------------------------

- Enter or Space: throw. Then arrows (or the mouse) to a lit man, Enter to
  pick it up, a lit square to put it down; the middle stands for men still
  to come out. P: leave the rest of the throws unused.
- The panel shows the throw as it fell and the throws still to use (1* a
  starting 1 or grace).
- When the game is over, Enter plays again.
- M: menu; F1: this help; Q: quit.
- O: the ornament off and on (the flower of a painted pachisi board,
  Udaipur, about 1868); --plain starts without it.
