Alquerque and Fanorona
Asciiverse
2026-10-25

Two games on boards of points joined by lines: along every row and column,
and along the diagonals through every other point. A man moves one step
along a line to an empty point. In alquerque a man takes as in draughts,
leaping over an enemy man next to it to the empty point beyond, and may leap
on. In fanorona a man takes by moving towards an enemy man (approach) or
away from one next to it (withdrawal), and the whole unbroken line of enemy
men in that direction goes; it may take again in a new direction. Whoever
takes every man of the other side, or leaves him no move, wins. The rules in
short follow; the full rules, the sources and the history are in RULES.txt
and the rulebook.

Alquerque de doze, after Alfonso X
----------------------------------

- 5 by 5 points, 12 men each; only the centre c3 is empty. White begins
  (Alfonso throws dice for it; our choice).
- A move: one step along a line, any way, to an empty point.
- Taking: leap along a line over an enemy man next to you onto the empty
  point beyond; take him; you may leap on over more, turning as you like,
  and stop when you like. Taking is not compulsory.
- Whoever loses all his men, or has no move, loses (no move: our choice). A
  third repetition, or 50 turns with nothing taken, draws (our choice).

Alquerque, after Bell
---------------------

- As Alfonso’s game, but taking is compulsory, and a man that has taken must
  take again while it can (the huff played as compulsion: our choice).
- No man steps backwards: only forwards, diagonally forwards or sideways.
  Leaps may go any way.
- No man may come back to a point it has stood on before.
- A man on the other side’s back row moves only to take.

Fanorona, today’s rules
-----------------------

- 9 by 5 points, 22 men each; the middle row alternates, the centre e3
  empty. White begins.
- A move (paika): one step along a line to an empty point.
- Taking: a step that ends next to an enemy man, moving towards him, takes
  him and every enemy man straight behind him, up to the first empty point
  or man of yours (approach); a step away from an enemy man next to you
  takes him and the men behind him in the same way (withdrawal). If a step
  could do both, you choose.
- If you can take, you must. Then the same man may take again, as often as
  it can, but never twice running in the same direction, and never onto a
  point it has stood on this turn. You may stop at any time.
- Whoever takes every enemy man, or leaves him no move, wins. A third
  repetition draws; so do 50 turns with nothing taken (our choice).

Fanorona, after Montgomery
--------------------------

- As today’s rules, but the very first turn of the game takes once only, and
  a man taking again may not go straight back along the line it came. It may
  take twice running in the same direction, and may come back to a point of
  this turn.

Vela
----

- The game after a defeat. Black, who won, may not take at all until he has
  lost seventeen men; then his last five play on as usual.
- White, who lost, moves first. Until then each of White’s captures takes
  one man only, the nearest, and ends the turn; White must take when he can.
- Then everything is as in fanorona after Montgomery.

Fanoron-dimy
------------

- Fanorona on the board of five by five: 12 men each, the middle row Black,
  White, empty, Black, White. Today’s rules.

Playing it in the terminal
--------------------------

- Arrows: move the cursor; Enter or Space: pick up a man, then put it on a
  lit point. A turn that takes again goes on from where the man stands:
  choose the next lit point, or press Esc (or point at the man) to stop,
  where stopping is allowed.
- If one step could take two lines, the men of both are lit: point at the
  line to take.
- The men a step would take are marked ✕ when the cursor is on where it
  goes. When the game is over, Enter plays again.
- M: menu; F1: this help; Q: quit.
