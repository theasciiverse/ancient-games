Shatranj
Asciiverse
2026-10-25

Chess before the queen and the bishop. Eight by eight squares; each side has
a shah (king), a firzan (counsellor), two fils (elephants), two faras
(horses), two rukhs (chariots) and eight baidaqs (foot soldiers). The shah,
the faras and the rukh move as the king, the knight and the rook do today;
the firzan steps one square diagonally; the fil leaps exactly two squares
diagonally, over anything; the baidaq steps one square forward and takes one
diagonally forward, and on the far row becomes a firzan. No castling, no
double step. You win by mate, by taking every man of his but the shah
(baring him), or, in shatranj, by leaving him without a move. The rules in
short follow; the full rules, the sources and the history are in RULES.txt
and the rulebook.

Shatranj
--------

- Red moves first (our choice; the manuscripts give Red the move).
- Mate wins. A stalemated shah (no legal move, not in check) loses.
- Baring wins: take every man of his but the shah while keeping one of
  yours. But if he can take your last man on his very next move, he may, and
  both bare is a draw.
- A third repetition of a position is a draw; so are 70 moves each with
  nothing taken and no baidaq moved (our choice).

Shatranj, the Medinese victory
------------------------------

- As shatranj, but whoever first bares the other’s shah wins at once, even
  if the bared shah could take the last man back.

Chaturanga, the Indian rules
----------------------------

- The men move as in shatranj.
- A stalemated shah wins: the side left without a move, not in check, has
  won (al-’Adli’s Indian rule).
- Whoever first bares the other’s shah wins (the Medinese rule, which
  al-’Adli calls Indian).
- Draws as in shatranj (our choice).

The elephant in the corner
--------------------------

- Chaturanga, the Indian rules, but the elephant stands in the corner and
  leaps exactly two squares along a rank or file, over anything. The rukh
  (chariot) stands on c1 and f1.

Al-Biruni’s elephant
--------------------

- Chaturanga, the Indian rules, but the elephant steps one square diagonally
  or one straight forward: “the trunk and the four feet”.

Playing it in the terminal
--------------------------

- Arrows: move the cursor; Enter or Space: pick up a man, then put it on a
  lit square; Esc: let go. Or click.
- The panel shows the last moves: letter, from, to, x for a capture, =F for
  a baidaq made firzan, + for check.
- When the game is over, Enter plays again.
- M: menu; F1: this help; Q: quit.
